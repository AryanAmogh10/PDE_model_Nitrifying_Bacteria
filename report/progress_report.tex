\section{Overview}

This report documents the numerical solver work performed on the codebase at
the repository above, which implements the six-stage finite-volume PDE model
of Freingruber, Gonzalez-Cabaleiro and Yoldas (arXiv:2512.13156) for three
competing nitrifying bacterial species --- ammonia oxidizers (AOB), nitrite
oxidizers (NOB), and comammox (CMX) --- growing in a spherical/cylindrical
biofilm granule. Three bacterial density fields $u_{\mathrm{AOB}},
u_{\mathrm{NOB}}, u_{\mathrm{CMX}}$ (slow, parabolic
advection--diffusion--reaction) are coupled to four substrate concentration
fields $c_{\mathrm{NH_4}}, c_{\mathrm{NO_2}}, c_{\mathrm{NO_3}}, c_{\mathrm{O_2}}$
(fast, quasi-steady elliptic) via the six-stage pipeline in
Table~\ref{tab:pipeline}, which mirrors the repository's \code{README.md}
exactly.

This document is written to double as a reading guide to the repository: every
claim below names the specific module and, where applicable, the exact
\code{tests/} function that establishes it, so the codebase can be inspected
directly against each statement. Every claim is labelled \verified{}
(independently confirmed with numbers, reproduced within this project),
\consistent{} (internally self-consistent, not cross-checked against an
external ground truth), or \assumed{} (a modelling choice taken on faith from
the source material, not independently verified).

\section{Chronology of Bugs Found and Fixed}

\subsection{Death term: constant vs.\ density-dependent (\code{parabolic.py})}
The original implementation used a constant death rate $-\hat b_i$. Direct
visual inspection of the rendered arXiv PDF (eq.\ 2.1/2.5) confirmed the
death term is density-dependent, $-\hat b_i \hat\rho$, $\hat\rho=\sum_j u_j$.
\verified{}: an earlier PDF text-extraction pass had silently dropped the
$\rho$ subscript. Fixed in \code{parabolic.py}; regression test
\test{tests/test\_parabolic.py::test\_death\_term\_is\_density\_dependent\_not\_constant}
checks that the per-step growth ratio strictly decreases under ample uniform
substrate (a constant death term would give exactly exponential growth
instead).

\subsection{Row-0 (domain-centre) reaction zeroing (\code{elliptic.py}, \code{relaxation.py})}
An earlier version zeroed the reaction contribution at the $r=0$ row in both
\code{elliptic.py} (\code{\_residual}, \code{\_assemble\_global}) and
\code{relaxation.py}, mistakenly treating $r=0$ as a boundary row; it is a
genuine interior point using a symmetric stencil. \verified{} via a hand-built
reference-Jacobian comparison and the convergence-order study in
Section~\ref{sec:convergence}. Fixed by restoring the row-0 reaction term in
both modules.

\subsection{Near-centre finite-volume cell-volume approximation (\code{elliptic.py::build\_laplacian})}
\label{sec:fv-fix}
Even after the row-0 fix, the convergence-order benchmark
(Section~\ref{sec:convergence}) still showed only $\sim$1.7--1.9th order with
reactions active. Root cause: interior rows near the centre ($i{=}1,2,3,\dots$)
used the finite-volume shortcut $V_i \approx r_i^p h$, whose relative error is
$O(h^2/r_i^2)$ --- 7.7\% at $i{=}1$, falling off for larger $i$. Not a row-0
bug (row 0's own formula was already exact); an under-resolved volume
normalisation on the first few rows adjacent to the centre. \verified{} by
rederiving \code{build\_laplacian} to use the exact cell volume
$V_i=(r_e^{p+1}-r_w^{p+1})/(p+1)$ at every row, confirming exact mass
conservation to machine precision for slab, cylindrical, and spherical
geometries, and a flattened (no longer centre-peaked) spatial error profile.

\subsection{Neumann boundary residual target hardcoded to zero}
\label{sec:neumann-bug}
Discovered during Neumann boundary-condition exploration
(Section~\ref{sec:neumann-tests}): three functions ---
\code{elliptic.py::\_residual}, \code{elliptic.py::solve\_picard}, and
\code{relaxation.py::solve\_relaxation} --- all hardcoded the Neumann row's
residual/RHS target to \code{0.0} instead of \code{-value} (the flux value
passed to \code{apply\_bc}, which itself correctly built the matrix row).
Effect: any \code{bc\_type='neumann'} solve, in any of the three solvers,
silently converged to a zero-flux solution regardless of the requested
nonzero flux. \verified{}: fixed in all three locations; regression test
\test{tests/test\_elliptic\_closed\_form.py::test\_nonzero\_flux\_neumann\_matches\_closed\_form}
added (Section~\ref{sec:neumann-fix-verify}).

\subsection{Plausibility-bound sign bug (\code{elliptic.py::solve\_newton})}
While fixing the spurious-root problem (Section~\ref{sec:problemAB}), an
upper concentration bound \code{c\_max = c\_max\_factor * max(c\_inf.values())}
was added. This breaks when \code{c\_inf} values are \emph{negative} ---
legitimate for a Neumann influx --- since \code{max(-0.3,0,0,0)=0.0} collapses
the bound to zero. \verified{}: fixed to \code{max(abs(v) for v in
c\_inf.values())}; the original spurious-root guard reconfirmed still holding,
and normal positive-\code{c\_inf} Dirichlet operation confirmed unaffected.

\subsection{Fallback diagnostic mislabeling and tolerance mismatch}
A relaxation-based fallback added to \code{solve\_newton} for genuinely
degenerate (singular-Jacobian) states could silently substitute a relaxation
answer while \code{slowfast.py::solve\_c\_given\_u} labelled the step
\code{"newton"}, using \code{solve\_relaxation}'s default tolerance
($10^{-9}$) rather than the caller's. \verified{}: \code{solve\_newton} now
returns \code{(C, history, method)} with an honest method string; the inner
fallback targets the caller's own \code{tol}; a documented, independently
confirmed-reachable outer backstop remains in \code{solve\_c\_given\_u} for a
distinct failure mode.

\subsection{Stage 3: \code{test\_elliptic.py}, \code{test\_elliptic\_closed\_form.py}, \code{test\_reaction\_terms.py}}

$u_{\mathrm{NOB}}{=}u_{\mathrm{CMX}}{=}0$, $K_{\mathrm{NH_4}}\!\gg\! c_\infty$

$0=\nabla^2 c-\kappa^2 c$, $c(1){=}c_\infty$, $\partial_r c(0){=}0$, with

\text{slab: } c_\infty\frac{\cosh(\kappa r)}{\cosh\kappa}, \quad
\text{cylindrical: } c_\infty\frac{I_0(\kappa r)}{I_0(\kappa)}, \quad
\text{spherical: } c_\infty\frac{\sinh(\kappa r)}{r\sinh\kappa}.
\]

\subsection{Stage 3 Neumann boundary conditions}
\label{sec:neumann-tests}
Three sub-cases were explored (a purpose-built test wrapper was used to
bypass a separate, still-open API quirk in which \code{solve\_newton}'s
standard entry point reuses \code{c\_inf\_hat} as the Neumann flux magnitude,
so a genuine ``zero-flux'' request cannot be expressed for a substrate whose
bulk \code{c\_inf} is itself nonzero):
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item \textbf{Zero-flux, reaction off.} \verified{}: initial concentration
returned exactly unchanged (0 Newton iterations, residual $4.5\times10^{-13}$),
independently for all four substrates.
\item \textbf{Nonzero-flux, reaction off.} \verified{} to correctly
\emph{fail}: no steady state exists (Fredholm solvability violated); NH$_4$
diverges to $\sim5\times10^{11}$; confirmed via smallest singular value of the
pure-Neumann Laplacian block, $3.2\times10^{-15}$.
\item \textbf{Zero-flux, reaction on.} Found the spurious-root bug: undamped
Newton converged to NH$_4\to100.5$, NO$_3\to5416$ --- orders of magnitude
above anything the sealed system could supply.
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Fix and re-verification (Problems A and B)}
\label{sec:problemAB}
\textbf{Problem A} (spurious root): an upper bound \code{c\_max} = 5$\times$
(largest-magnitude \code{c\_inf} entry), enforced jointly with residual
decrease during backtracking. \verified{}: the spurious root is now
unreachable; Newton instead converges to $C\equiv0$ (legitimate, since
Monod$(0){=}0$). \textbf{Problem B} (genuine degeneracy, not a bug): once a
substrate is driven to exactly 0 everywhere under zero-flux Neumann, the
Jacobian can become exactly singular; detected and handed to
\code{relaxation.solve\_relaxation}. \verified{}: forcing the exact degenerate
state directly confirmed the fallback engages, converging to residual
$3.18\times10^{-12}$.

\subsection{Neumann fix verification pass (independent re-check)}
\label{sec:neumann-fix-verify}
A subsequent, independent verification pass re-derived every claim with fresh
numbers rather than re-running the same tests and reporting green:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Code confirmed.} All three lines quoted directly from current
source, each reading \code{c\_inf[sub] if bc\_type=="dirichlet" else
-c\_inf[sub]}; exhaustive \code{grep} confirmed zero remaining hardcoded
\code{0.0} Neumann targets anywhere in \code{nitrifiers/}.
\item \textbf{Scope honestly bounded.} The regression test
(\test{test\_nonzero\_flux\_neumann\_matches\_closed\_form}) uses a
\emph{mixed}-BC construction (Dirichlet for O$_2$/NO$_2$/NO$_3$, nonzero
Neumann for NH$_4$ only) --- \textbf{not} the standard
\code{solve\_newton(bc\_type='neumann')} entry point applied to all four
coupled substrates at once. That entry point was tested directly on
genuinely-coupled configurations and found \unverified{} --- in fact actively
broken: one configuration diverged to residual $2.15\times10^9$ (after
exhausting its own internal relaxation fallback's 5000-step budget); a further
batch of three configurations did not complete within 100 seconds. Reported
as an open issue in \code{README.md} $\rightarrow$ ``Known limitations,'' not
smoothed over.
\item \textbf{Independent flux check.} Boundary flux recomputed by finite
difference directly from the returned \code{C} array (not any internal
solver quantity): requested $-0.3$, computed $-0.30000000$, difference
$1.1\times10^{-14}$.
\item \textbf{Full regression.} All 8 files in \code{tests/} re-run and
confirmed passing after every fix in this section.
\end{itemize}
The three follow-on fallback-transparency claims were likewise independently
re-confirmed: all six \code{solve\_newton} call sites (listed in
Table~\ref{tab:callsites}) correctly unpack the new 3-tuple, both statically
and at runtime; a run at \code{tol}=$10^{-11}$ (tighter than the old
hardcoded $10^{-9}$ default) reached residual $7.28\times10^{-12}$, past
where the old default would have stopped, proving the caller's tolerance is
genuinely used; and both the inner (\code{"newton\_inner\_relax\_fallback"})
and outer (\code{"outer\_relaxation\_backstop"}) layers were shown
independently reachable via two distinct, deliberately constructed trigger
conditions.

\subsection{Stage 4: \code{test\_relaxation.py}}
\test{test\_relaxation\_matches\_newton\_across\_densities\_and\_presets} runs
all three presets $\times$ four uniform densities
($10^{-4},0.01,0.05,0.5$). \verified{}, post Section~\ref{sec:fv-fix}:
agreement of $8.7\times10^{-12}$ to $7.8\times10^{-11}$ across all twelve
combinations --- roughly five orders tighter than the $10^{-6}$ threshold in
the test file. Relaxation converges in $\sim$20--22 pseudo-steps regardless
of preset/density.
\test{test\_relaxation\_reproduces\_anoxic\_core} confirms O$_2\approx
2.3\times10^{-30}$ at the centre, $0.400$ at the rim, strictly monotonic.
\test{test\_relaxation\_converges\_and\_stays\_physical} confirms
non-negativity and residual $<10^{-8}$.

\subsection{Stage 5: \code{test\_parabolic.py}}
Nine tests (Table~\ref{tab:testmap}, row 5) cover exact mass conservation of
diffusion (\test{test\_diffusion\_operator\_is\_exactly\_mass\_conservative});
advection-operator conservation convergence
(\test{test\_advection\_operator\_converges\_to\_mass\_conservative});
substrate-gated, non-negative growth

(\test{test\_stable\_and\_bounded\_at\_large\_dt}); the analytic

(\test{test\_sharp\_front\_newton\_converges\_to\_tolerance}) --- the regime in

\subsection{Stage 6: \code{test\_slowfast.py}, plus dedicated exploration scripts}
\test{test\_slow\_loop\_runs\_and\_stays\_physical\_on\_all\_presets},
\test{test\_relaxation\_fallback\_is\_available\_and\_keeps\_residual\_low},
\test{test\_zonation\_persists\_through\_the\_slow\_loop}, and
\test{test\_bacterial\_mass\_grows\_over\_the\_slow\_loop\_when\_substrate\_is\_ample}
together confirm the coupled elliptic+parabolic loop runs cleanly, stays
physical (non-negative, bounded mass), and preserves rim-to-core O$_2$
zonation over 20--30 slow steps. A separate 100-step \code{eloi}-preset run
(outside the pytest suite) confirmed clean Newton convergence throughout
(9--10 iterations/step, residuals $\sim 10^{-12}$) and mass growth
$0.00423\to0.0118$, growth-ratio std.\ $10^{-4}$ over the final 10 steps.

\paragraph{CMX-vs-AOB competitive dominance (dedicated diagnostic, not in \code{tests/}).}
Motivated by CMX density counter-intuitively peaking at the anoxic core.
Tracking CMX density and O$_2$ at $r{=}0$ over all 100 steps showed CMX core
density \emph{decaying} $-10.58\%$ net --- \verified{} expected commensalism
behaviour (cross-diffusion pushes biomass inward, where it then starves), not
a bug. A full Stage 6 sweep over NH$_4$ feed ($1.0\to3\times10^{-4}$,
dimensionless) found CMX can overtake AOB only at NH$_4\le0.0003$--$0.0004$
(0.15--0.2 mg/L, vs.\ \code{eloi}'s real 500 mg/L feed --- factor $\sim$3000
lower), margin 1.0008--1.005, both species stagnant across the crossover.
\verified{} as genuine but practically unreachable under any realistic feed.

\subsection{Closed-form catalogue (documentation, cross-referenced against tests)}
Extended to Neumann boundary conditions with linear reaction, all three
geometries ($A\cosh$, $AI_0$, $A\sinh/r$, $A$ fixed by the flux). \verified{}
against the numerical solver, errors $4.6$--$8.1\times10^{-4}$ (consistent
with the documented first-order one-sided Neumann discretization). The full
nonlinear Monod case --- what \code{toy}/\code{rebeca}/\code{eloi} actually
use --- has \textbf{no known closed form for any geometry or boundary
condition}, which is why the project's validation strategy throughout relies
on the deep-linear-limit reduction, mass conservation, and cross-solver
agreement rather than a fully nonlinear closed form.

\section{Known Limitations (as stated in \code{README.md})}
\begin{itemize}
\item Picard requires a center-row RHS workaround to converge under
\code{eloi}'s reaction stiffness --- a property of the fixed-point map
(confirmed via Jacobian/eigenvalue analysis), not the discretization; Picard
is never relied on as a primary solver.
\item \code{solve\_newton(bc\_type='neumann')} remains unreliable for
genuinely multi-substrate-coupled configurations --- diverges or hangs. Does
not affect Stage 6 (always Dirichlet).
\item \code{c\_inf\_hat} is reused as both the Dirichlet value and the
Neumann flux magnitude, so a zero-flux request cannot be expressed for a
substrate with nonzero bulk concentration without a custom wrapper.
\item \code{solve\_newton}'s return signature is a 3-tuple
\code{(C, history, method)}; any new caller must unpack three values.
\item Several preset cleaning decisions remain \assumed{} rather than
verified against source (documented in \code{params.py}), notably the
\code{rebeca} $\div100$ yield rescale and \code{A\_OVER\_D\_RATIO}.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
The codebase's numerical core has been subjected to an extensive validation
campaign: closed-form ground-truth benchmarks across three geometries and two
boundary-condition types, high-precision (50-digit) floating-point-artifact-free
convergence confirmation, exact mass conservation, independent two-solver
cross-validation to $\sim10^{-11}$, analytic Jacobian verification, and
targeted stress-testing that itself surfaced and required fixing four genuine
bugs beyond the original death-term error. Every fix was independently
re-verified with numbers after the fact (Table~\ref{tab:testmap},
Section~\ref{sec:neumann-fix-verify}), and two claims that could not be
substantiated --- the production multi-substrate Neumann entry point and full
Picard robustness --- are reported openly as \code{README.md} limitations
rather than folded into a blanket ``resolved.''
